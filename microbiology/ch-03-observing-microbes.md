## Summary

Microbes need small units. Table 3.1: 1 μm = 10⁻⁶ m. 1 nm = 10⁻⁹ m. So 1 μm = 1,000 nm.

- A 10 μm cell is 1 × 10⁻⁵ m, which is 10,000 nm. The slide asks this and leaves the blank. The table is enough to calculate it.
- The quiz: 100 nm = 0.1 μm.

Compound light path: illuminator → condenser → specimen → objective lenses → ocular lenses. The image is magnified twice. Total magnification = objective × ocular. A 100× objective and a 10× ocular = 1,000×.

Resolution is the ability of lenses to distinguish two points. A microscope with 0.4 nm resolving power can distinguish two points at least 0.4 nm apart. The best resolution of a light microscope on the slide is 0.2 μm (2,000×). Shorter wavelengths give greater resolution. The word “Shorter” is printed in red.

Brightfield: direct light, dark objects on a bright background, staining increases contrast. Darkfield: an opaque disk, only reflected light reaches the eyepiece, edges against a dark background. Fluorescence uses UV or other short wavelengths. The specimen emits visible light. Fluorochromes are fluorescent dyes on antibodies. Confocal microscopy is listed as another UV method.

Electron microscopy uses a beam of electrons for objects smaller than about 0.2 μm and gives greater resolution. TEM: ultrathin sections, 2-D internal image, 10,000–100,000×, resolution 2.5 nm. SEM: surface scan, 3-D view, 1,000–10,000×, resolution 20 nm. The quiz says the electron microscope has the highest magnification and greatest resolution of the options given.

A smear is the organism spread on a slide. Fixing means passing the slide through a flame. It kills, attaches, and preserves structures. The quiz says fixing is not done to give bacteria a negative charge.

At pH 7, bacterial cells are slightly negative and attract positive ions. Stains are salts. The colored ion is the chromophore. Basic dyes have a cation (+) chromophore: crystal violet, methylene blue, safranin. Acidic dyes have an anion (−) chromophore and are used in negative staining, so the cells stay colorless. The quiz says acidic dyes are negative ions and are repelled by most bacteria.

Simple stains show size, shape, and arrangement. Differential stains are the Gram stain and the acid-fast stain. Special stains are capsule (negative), endospore, and flagella.

Gram procedure on the figure: crystal violet (60 sec) → iodine (60 sec) → 95% ethyl alcohol (5–10 sec) → safranin (45 sec). Gram-positive cells stay purple or blue. Gram-negative cells are decolorized and then stain red or pink. The quiz calls the decolorizer alcohol-acetone and uses the order 4-2-3-1 (crystal violet, iodine, alcohol-acetone, safranin). If alcohol-acetone is omitted, Gram-negative cells stay purple.

Acid-fast cells stain red because of a thick outer lipid layer, mycolic acid. Primary stain is carbolfuchsin. Decolorizer is acid alcohol. Counterstain is methylene blue. Non-acid-fast cells stain blue. Examples: *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*, *Mycobacterium leprae*. They grow slowly because nutrient exchange is slow.

Named cells: *Staphylococcus aureus* is a Gram-positive coccus in clusters. *Streptococcus pyogenes* is a Gram-positive coccus in chains. *Streptococcus pneumoniae* is a Gram-positive lancet-shaped coccus in pairs. *Escherichia coli* is a Gram-negative rod. *Neisseria gonorrhoeae* (slide spelling: Neissseria gonorhoeae) is a Gram-negative coccus in pairs.

## 2. High-priority points

Likely exam content.

- Unit conversion between m, μm, and nm. 100 nm = 0.1 μm.
- Total magnification is objective times ocular.
- Electron microscopy has greater resolution than light microscopy. TEM is internal and 2-D. SEM is surface and 3-D.
- Fixing kills, attaches, and preserves. It does not give the cell a negative charge.
- Basic dye = positive chromophore. Acidic dye = negative chromophore, and it is repelled.
- Gram order and colors. Skipping the decolorizer leaves Gram-negative cells purple.
- Acid alcohol is the acid-fast decolorizer. Acid-fast cells stay red. Non-acid-fast cells turn blue.
- Purple cocci in clusters on a Gram slide are Gram-positive cocci in clusters.
- Mycolic acid and *Mycobacterium* return in Chapters 4 and 7.
