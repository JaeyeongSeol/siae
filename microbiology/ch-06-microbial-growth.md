## Summary

Growing a bacterium follows the five I’s: inoculate, incubate, isolate, inspect, identify. A sample can come from skin, blood, water, food, or any object. Inoculation places a sterile loop into a medium with nutrients. The medium can be liquid, solid, or a live organism. The slide asks for an example of a microbe grown in a living organism and does not answer. Incubation supplies temperature and gas. Isolation is the result of inoculation and incubation, then subculture, until the culture is pure. Inspection covers colony shape, size, color, and stain. Identification uses morphology, differential staining, and biochemical tests for enzymes.

Chemical requirements are carbon, oxygen, nitrogen, sulfur, phosphorus, and trace elements. Physical requirements are temperature, pH, and osmotic pressure. Radiation and atmospheric pressure are also named.

Autotrophs use CO2 as the carbon source and inorganic salts as the nitrogen source. Heterotrophs use organic carbon. Nitrogen is used to make amino acids and proteins. The source of nitrogen is asked and not answered. Sulfur is used in amino acids and in thiamine and biotin. Sources are sulfate or H2S. Phosphorus is used in DNA, RNA, ATP, and membranes. The source is inorganic phosphate.

Oxygen divides microorganisms into five groups. Toxic products named on the slide are the superoxide radical, hydrogen peroxide, and the hydroxyl radical. Aerobes make protective enzymes.

| Group | Slide wording | Where growth is drawn | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Obligate aerobe | Absolute requirement for O2. Respiration. SOD and catalase | Top of the tube | *Micrococcus*, *Corynebacterium*, *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, *Bordetella pertussis*, *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* |
| Facultative anaerobe | Grows with or without O2. Prefers O2. Respiration with O2 and fermentation without it. SOD and catalase | Throughout, heavier at the top | *E. coli*, *Salmonella*, *Shigella*, *S. aureus*, *S. cerevisiae* |
| Obligate anaerobe | No SOD and no catalase. Mostly fermentation | Bottom only. Growth ceases in oxygen | *C. tetani*, *C. botulinum*, *Clostridioides difficile*, *Bacteroides* |
| Aerotolerant anaerobe | Does not require O2. SOD, and some make catalase | Evenly through the tube | *Lactobacillus*, *Streptococcus* |
| Microaerophile | Higher CO2 and lower O2 than air. The tube label says about 2–10% | A band below the surface | *Neisseria gonorrhoeae*, *Neisseria meningitidis* |

Table 6.1 says obligate anaerobes lack the enzymes that neutralize harmful forms of oxygen. Aerotolerant cells have SOD and tolerate oxygen. Microaerophiles produce lethal amounts of toxic oxygen products if exposed to normal air.

Each organism has a minimum, optimum, and maximum temperature. Those are the cardinal temperatures.

| Group | Minimum | Optimum | Maximum |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Psychrophiles | −10°C | 15°C | 20°C |
| Psychrotrophs | 0°C | 25°C | 30°C |
| Mesophiles | 15°C | 20–40°C | 45°C |
| Thermophiles | 45°C | 55–70°C | 80°C |
| Hyperthermophiles | 65°C | 85–95°C | 105°C |

The slide spelling is “Hyperthemophiles.” Psychrophiles are in the deep sea and polar regions and are rarely a food problem. Psychrotrophs spoil refrigerated food. Mesophiles are the common cause of food spoilage and disease. Thermophiles cannot grow below 40°C and are not called a public health problem. Hyperthermophiles grow near hydrothermal vents, including 121°C, and are not called a public health problem.

Acidophiles grow best from pH 0 to 5.5. Neutrophiles grow best from pH 5.5 to 8. Alkalophiles grow best from pH 8.5 to 11.5. *Helicobacter pylori* lives in the stomach under the mucus layer. How it survives that pH is asked and not answered.

High salt or sugar causes plasmolysis and is used in food preservation. Halophiles grow optimally above 0.2 M. Non-halophiles are below 0.2 M NaCl. Extreme (obligate) halophiles require more than 2 M, which the slide calls 30% salt. “Facultative halophiles — explain?” is asked and not answered. Hypertonic, hypotonic, and isotonic are asked and not defined.

A culture medium is nutrients prepared for microbial growth. Sterile means no living microbes. An inoculum is the microbes introduced into the medium. A culture is the microbes growing in or on the medium. Forms: broth, plate, slant, deep. Agar is a complex polysaccharide used as a solidifying agent. It is generally not metabolized. It liquefies at 100°C and solidifies at about 40°C.

Chemically defined media have every component known and quantified, for example minimum media. Complex media have some unknown or unquantified components, for example nutrient agar and nutrient broth.

Enriched media favor selected bacteria, for example blood agar and chocolate agar. *Haemophilus influenzae* is shown on chocolate agar as a fastidious bacterium. Differential media distinguish bacteria on the same plate, for example hemolysis on blood agar. Selective media encourage some bacteria and suppress others, for example MacConkey agar. MacConkey is both: it selects Gram-negative cells and differentiates lactose fermenters. Mannitol salt agar selects Gram-positive cells and differentiates mannitol fermenters.

Bacterial growth is an increase in the number of cells, not in cell size. Bacteria divide by binary fission. Budding, conidiospores, and fragmentation are named as alternatives. Generation time is the time required for a cell to divide, or for a population to double. The average for bacteria is 1–3 hours. *E. coli* is 20 minutes. In 20 generations, about 7 hours, 1 cell becomes 1 million cells.

| Phase | Slide wording |
| --- | --- |
| Lag | Making new enzymes for a new medium. The figure says intense activity preparing for growth, with no increase in population |
| Log | Exponential growth. Most sensitive to drugs and radiation. The figure says the increase is from binary fission in bacteria or mitosis in yeast |
| Stationary | Nutrients become limiting or wastes become toxic. Death rate equals division rate |
| Death | Death exceeds division. The figure says the population decreases at a logarithmic rate |

The quiz sentence “grows best in the presence of oxygen but can also grow in the absence of oxygen” is a facultative anaerobe.

## 2. High-priority points

Likely exam content.

- The five oxygen groups, the tube positions, and which ones make SOD and catalase. The last quiz question was the facultative-anaerobe definition.
- Cardinal temperatures, especially which group spoils refrigerated food (psychrotrophs) and which group causes most disease (mesophiles).
- MacConkey selects Gram-negative cells and differentiates lactose fermenters. Mannitol salt agar selects Gram-positive cells.
- Growth is more cells, not bigger cells. The log phase is the drug-sensitive phase.
- *E. coli* doubles in 20 minutes.
- The source of nitrogen, facultative halophiles, osmosis words, how *H. pylori* survives acid, and an example of growth in a living host are asked and not answered.
