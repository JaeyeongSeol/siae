## Summary

Sterilization is the destruction or removal of all forms of microbial life from an object. Methods named: filtration, high temperature, incineration, ultraviolet or gamma irradiation, ethylene oxide, chlorine dioxide, steam sterilization, and filtration again under mechanical processes.

Disinfection uses chemicals to kill or inhibit microorganisms that cause disease. It does not kill all microorganisms. Disinfectants are usually toxic to human tissues. Chemicals listed: chlorine, phenols, biphenols such as chlorhexidine, alcohols, iodine, aldehydes, quats, and also ozone and UV light.

Sanitization is related to disinfection, but it only reduces the population to acceptable standards, such as restaurant utensils. Antisepsis uses chemicals on living tissue. It is still toxic. Common antiseptics: alcohol, iodine, chlorhexidine, and heavy metals (silver, mercury).

“-cide” means the agent kills. A biocide or germicide kills microorganisms. A virocide inactivates viruses. A fungicide kills fungi. “-static” means the agent inhibits growth. A bacteriostatic agent inhibits the growth of bacteria. Sepsis is bacterial contamination. Asepsis is the absence of significant contamination. Aseptic techniques are methods that minimize contamination.

A larger population takes longer to kill. Different populations have different sensitivity. Slide 8 says a higher concentration has a greater effect. Longer exposure kills more organisms. High temperature increases killing. Organic material, such as sewage and biofilms, decreases killing. Spores are more resistant than vegetative cells.

Slide 10 says “The higher the conc. the longer it takes.” Slide 8 says the higher concentration has the greater effect. The slides do not reconcile those two sentences.

Resistance, most to least, on the figure: prions, endospores of bacteria, mycobacteria, cysts of protozoa, vegetative protozoa, Gram-negative bacteria, fungi including most fungal spores, viruses without envelopes, Gram-positive bacteria, viruses with lipid envelopes.

Matching sentences: Gram-negative cells are more resistant than Gram-positive cells. *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* is the most resistant Gram-negative. Acid-fast cells are more resistant than non-acid-fast cells. *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* is resistant to aqueous bactericides. The slide asks why and does not answer. Endospores are the most resistant bacterial forms. Naked viruses are more resistant than enveloped viruses. The slide asks why and does not answer. Cysts are more resistant than trophozoites. *Cryptosporidium* cysts resist chlorine in water.

Bacteria die at a constant rate. Thermal death point is the lowest temperature that kills all microbes in a liquid culture in 10 minutes. Thermal death time is the time required to kill all bacteria in a liquid culture at a given temperature. Decimal reduction time is the time taken to kill 90% of the population at a given temperature.

Moist heat is more effective than dry heat. Moist heat kills by coagulation of protein. Steam is more effective than boiling water. Endospores resist boiling. Dry heat kills by oxidation and needs higher temperatures and a longer time. Incineration, flaming, and an oven at 170°C for 2 hours are the dry-heat examples.

An autoclave is a steam sterilizer. It works like a pressure cooker: saturate the chamber with steam, raise the temperature to 121°C, and apply 15 psi for 15 minutes. Steam condenses on the objects. Pressure is brought back to normal before the chamber is opened. Why that matters for liquids, and why it is unnecessary for solid objects, are asked and not answered. In canning, steam sterilization kills heat-resistant spores of *Clostridium botulinum*.

Pasteurization reduces the number of pathogens and does not change taste. Milk pasteurization is designed to kill *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*, called the most heat-resistant pathogen in milk. It also kills pathogenic and non-pathogenic *Streptococcus*, *Brucella*, and coliforms.

| Method | Numbers on the slide |
| --- | --- |
| Low-temperature hold (LTH) | 62.8°C for 30 minutes |
| High-temperature short-time (HTST) | 72°C for 15 seconds |
| Ultra-high temperature (UHT) | 5 second cycle: 74°C → 140°C → 74°C |

Filtration reduces microbes in liquids and air and sterilizes heat-sensitive materials. Two types: depth filters and membrane filters. Membrane filters are 0.1 mm thick, with pore sizes from 0.2 μm.

Refrigeration is bacteriostatic. Most pathogens do not grow, but *Listeria monocytogenes* grows in the refrigerator. Desiccation is bacteriostatic. Lyophilization preserves cultures. High salt or sugar is an osmotic method. Molds and yeasts are the most resistant to drying and high salt.

UV light is non-ionizing. It does not penetrate, and it can damage eyes and DNA. Ionizing radiation is gamma rays, X-rays, or high-energy electron beams. It causes mutations and death. Low-level ionizing radiation is used on spices and some meats and vegetables. High-energy radiation sterilizes medical supplies.

Disc dilution is the disinfectant test on the slide: inoculate a plate, add discs, incubate, and measure zones of inhibition.

Phenol was used by Joseph Lister. Lysol is a mixture of phenols. Hexachlorophene is a skin disinfectant, effective against *S. aureus*, and neurotoxic in rats. Triclosan was used in soaps and toothpaste and was banned in US soaps in 2017.

Chlorhexidine is a biguanide. Dentists use it. It is also a skin antiseptic, has low toxicity, and is used on skin and mucous membranes.

Iodine is used as a tincture (iodine plus alcohol) or an iodophor (iodine plus an organic carrier), such as Betadine. The slide says it is effective against all bacteria, spores, fungi, and viruses. Chlorine disinfects water. The active ingredient is hypochlorous acid. It is corrosive, inactivated by organic matter, and forms by-products linked to bladder cancer. Chlorine gas can form bleach in water. Chloramines are chlorine and ammonia.

Ethanol and isopropanol denature proteins and damage the cell membrane. They evaporate quickly. They are more active as 70% aqueous solutions than as 100%.

Heavy metals denature proteins: silver nitrate, mercury in some eye and lens products, copper sulfate as an algicide, and zinc in mouthwash and paints.

Surfactants decrease surface tension or damage lipid membranes. Soaps and detergents have cleaning value and little disinfectant value. Quats are microbiocidal, fungicidal, amoebicidal, and virucidal against enveloped viruses. They are more effective against Gram-positive cells. They are not effective against endospores or mycobacteria. *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* (slide spelling: Pseudomonas auriginosa) can grow in quats. The clinic note is *C. difficile*.

Ethylene oxide denatures proteins, penetrates well, and kills all microbes and spores, but it needs a long exposure. Hospitals use it as a chemical sterilant. Chlorine dioxide is short-lived and is used to sterilize buildings and contents, including anthrax endospores, and as an initial water treatment before chlorination.

Peroxygens interfere with metabolism, especially of anaerobes. Hydrogen peroxide is a common antiseptic. Peracetic acid is used to wash fruits and vegetables. Ozone is used by the GVRD as a primary water disinfectant so chlorine can be used at a lower concentration.

Food preservatives named: sulfur dioxide, sodium benzoate, sorbic acid, calcium propionate, sodium nitrate, and sodium nitrite. Antibiotics are “discussed latter.” The antimicrobials in this lecture are not for ingestion or injection to treat disease.

## 2. High-priority points

Likely exam content. These are the L6 objectives, and several connect to earlier lectures.

- Sterilization removes all microbial life. Disinfection does not, and it is for objects. Antisepsis is for living tissue. Sanitization only lowers the count to a standard.
- “-cide” kills. “-static” only stops growth. Refrigeration and drying are bacteriostatic.
- Resistance order, from prions and endospores down to enveloped viruses. Gram-negative cells are harder to kill than Gram-positive cells. Endospores outlast vegetative cells. Acid-fast cells outlast non-acid-fast cells.
- Autoclave: 121°C, 15 psi, 15 minutes. Pasteurization times: 62.8°C for 30 minutes, 72°C for 15 seconds, and the UHT cycle through 140°C.
- Alcohol is more active at 70% than at 100%.
- Quats fail against endospores, mycobacteria, and *Pseudomonas*.
- *Listeria* grows in the refrigerator. *Cryptosporidium* cysts resist chlorine.
- Why *M. tuberculosis* resists aqueous bactericides, why naked viruses are more resistant, and why the autoclave pressure is released before opening a liquid load are asked and not answered.
