## Summary

Microorganisms are organisms too small to be seen with the unaided eye. Few are pathogenic. The slide also lists food spoilage, decomposition, nitrogen incorporation, oxygen from photosynthesis, chemical products (ethanol, acetone, vitamins), fermented foods, and industrial products (cellulose, insulin, antibiotics).

The human microbiome (microbiota) is the group of microbes that live stably on or in the human body. An adult has 30 trillion body cells and harbors 40 trillion bacterial cells. Microbiota begin to be acquired before birth. They may colonize indefinitely, or only fleetingly (transient microbiota). They help maintain health, can prevent growth of pathogens, and may train the immune system.

Carl Woese (1978) described three domains based on cellular organization: Bacteria, Archaea, and Eukarya (protists, fungi, plants, and animals). Types also listed: bacteria, fungi, protozoans, algae, viruses.

Infection results from invasion of part of the body by a microorganism. Those microbes are called infectious, infectious agents, pathogens, or pathogenic microorganisms. This class studies bacteria, fungi, viruses, and protozoa, and also helminths and arthropods. Helminths and arthropods are not microbes. They are parasites and vectors. Algae are listed as non-infectious, with toxins.

Subdivisions: bacteriology, virology, mycology, parasitology (protozoans, worms, insects).

Spontaneous generation is the hypothesis that living organisms arise from non-living matter. Biogenesis is the hypothesis that living organisms arise from pre-existing life.

Pasteur bent the flask neck into an S-shape, then boiled the broth. The bend kept microbes out. The broth stayed free of microorganisms. The quiz says the curves excluded airborne bacteria.

The second golden age focused on treating disease: Ehrlich’s “magic bullet,” penicillin (Fleming, 1928), sulfonamides, and chemotherapy (synthetic drugs). The third golden age is molecular biology: genetic manipulations and genomics.

Three approaches: organismal (type of organism), body systems (body part infected), ecological (route of infection, including nosocomial and arthropod-borne).

## 2. High-priority points

Likely exam content. The slides repeat these, highlight them, or the quiz already asked them.

- Woese, 1978, three domains. L3 returns to the same system and bases it on rRNA.
- Spontaneous generation versus biogenesis, including which flask grew and which stayed clear.
- The swan-neck flask let air in and excluded airborne bacteria.
- Koch is the germ-theory match. Fleming is the antibiotic match. Ehrlich is the magic bullet. Hooke and van Leeuwenhoek observed cells. Hess is agar, not pure cultures. Pure cultures are Koch. Lister is aseptic surgery.
- Jenner and Linnaeus are on the “responsible” list, and these slides do not say what they did.

Other disease categories named as outside this class: inherited, nutritional deficiencies, physiological disorders, degenerative diseases, neoplastic diseases, mental disorders.
