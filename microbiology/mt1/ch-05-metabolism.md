## Summary

Metabolism is the sum of the chemical reactions in an organism. Catabolism breaks larger molecules down and provides energy and building blocks for anabolism. Anabolism uses energy and building blocks to build large molecules. The slide spelling is “Building od macromolecules.” A metabolic pathway is a sequence of enzymatically catalyzed chemical reactions in a cell.

A catalyst increases the rate of a chemical reaction without itself undergoing any permanent chemical change. Almost all enzymes are proteins. The slide asks what ribozymes are and does not answer. Enzymes lower activation energy, which is an energy barrier. Rate depends on temperature, pH, substrate concentration, enzyme concentration, and inhibitors.

ATP is the currency of chemical energy and is the intermediate between catabolism and anabolism. NADH and FADH2 are other energy forms. Carbohydrates such as glucose are especially useful. Respiration and fermentation are the processes used to produce ATP.

ATP is made by adding a phosphate to ADP. Substrate-level phosphorylation uses a high-energy phosphate already on a molecule. It takes place in glycolysis and the TCA cycle. Oxidative phosphorylation uses electrons from NADH and FADH2. Those electrons release energy in the electron transport chain, the energy builds a proton gradient, and the gradient drives ADP to ATP.

Cellular respiration uses glycolysis, the Krebs cycle, and the electron transport chain. The slide says the Krebs cycle and the electron transport chain require oxygen. Most of the energy is released during the electron transport chain and oxidative phosphorylation.

Glycolysis is the incomplete oxidation of glucose. One glucose (6 carbons) becomes 2 pyruvic acid (3 carbons each), a net of 2 ATP, and 2 NADH. The overall reaction on the slide invests 2 ATP and later shows 4 ATP, which is why the net is 2. Pyruvate is the starting material for the step that connects glycolysis to the Krebs cycle.

In the intermediate step, pyruvic acid is oxidized to acetyl CoA and CO2, and NADH is formed. One glucose makes 2 pyruvate and then 2 acetyl CoA. In aerobic respiration, the fate of pyruvic acid on the quiz is conversion to acetyl CoA.

The Krebs cycle completely oxidizes acetyl CoA to CO2 and forms some ATP plus NADH. The electron transport chain oxidizes NADH and FADH2, passes the electrons to O2, and makes water. The proton gradient drives ATP by chemiosmosis. Each NADH yields 3 ATP. Each FADH2 yields 2 ATP.

Complete aerobic oxidation of one glucose is 38 ATP on the slide:

| Pathway | ATP | NADH | FADH2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Glycolysis | 2 | 2 | 0 |
| Intermediate step | 0 | 2 | 0 |
| Krebs cycle | 2 | 6 | 2 |
| Carrier total | 4 | 10 × 3 = 30 | 2 × 2 = 4 |

If there is no oxygen, there is no Krebs cycle and no electron transport chain. The final electron acceptor is an organic molecule, for example pyruvic acid.

The scientific definition of fermentation is: releases energy from oxidation of organic molecules, does not require oxygen, and does not use the Krebs cycle or the electron transport chain. It does not completely break down sugars. Two general definitions are also printed: spoilage of food, and an industrial process that makes alcoholic beverages or acidic dairy products.

Alcoholic fermentation produces ethanol and CO2. Lactic acid fermentation produces lactic acid. Homolactic fermentation produces lactic acid only. Heterolactic fermentation produces lactic acid and other compounds.

The quiz definition among the options is “the partial oxidation of glucose with organic molecules serving as electron acceptors.” Yeast ferment to get energy when oxygen is limiting. If yeast are growing on starch and not making alcohol, the quiz answer is that oxygen is present. A bacterium that can grow with or without oxygen was keyed as using glycolysis, the Krebs cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation, which is the aerobic set of pathways.

The slides ask where fermentation and respiration occur in prokaryotes and eukaryotes and do not answer. The quiz key is in the quiz table below. That location row is the quiz key, not a sentence on the slides.

## 2. High-priority points

Likely exam content. This chapter is most of the metabolism quiz.

- Catabolism breaks down. Anabolism builds. ATP connects them.
- The drawn transfer from diphosphoglyceric acid to ADP is substrate-level phosphorylation. It is not oxidative phosphorylation.
- Substrate-level ATP comes from glycolysis and the Krebs cycle. Oxidative phosphorylation, and most of the cell’s ATP, come from the electron transport chain.
- Aerobic respiration on these slides is 38 ATP. NADH is 3. FADH2 is 2.
- Fermentation still makes ATP, in glycolysis. The statement “ATP is produced during respiration but not during fermentation” was false on the quiz.
- Homolactic means lactic acid only.
- Oxygen present means yeast that are growing do not have to make ethanol.
- “Proteins of the electron transport chain are located in the inner mitochondrial membrane of bacteria” was a false statement on the quiz. The slides do not place the bacterial chain in a mitochondrion.
- “What are ribozymes?”, “Why fermentation?”, and the cell location of fermentation and respiration are asked and not answered on the slides.
