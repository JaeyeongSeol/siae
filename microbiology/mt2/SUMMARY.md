## Chapter 7 — Control of microbial growth (L6)

**Terms:** Sterilization = destroy/remove **all** microbial life. Disinfection = kill/inhibit disease microbes; does **not** kill all; usually toxic to tissue. Sanitization = reduce to acceptable public-health standards. Antisepsis = chemicals on **living tissue**. **-cide** kills. **-static** inhibits. Sepsis = bacterial contamination. Asepsis = absence of significant contamination.

**Death rate factors:** population size, composition, concentration/intensity, time of exposure, environment (heat ↑ killing; organic matter ↓ killing), microbial characteristics (spores > vegetative).

**Resistance (most → least):** prions → endospores → mycobacteria → protozoan cysts → vegetative protozoa → Gram-negative → fungi → naked viruses → Gram-positive → enveloped viruses.

**Physical:** Moist heat coagulates protein (steam > boiling; autoclave **121°C, 15 psi, 15 min**). Dry heat oxidizes (oven **170°C, 2 h**). Pasteurization reduces pathogens without changing taste; milk target = *M. tuberculosis* (62.8°C/30 min; 72°C/15 sec; UHT through 140°C). Filtration for heat-labile liquids. Refrigeration bacteriostatic (*Listeria* can grow). UV non-penetrating; ionizing radiation mutates/kills.

**Chemical:** phenol/Lister, chlorhexidine, iodine, chlorine (hypochlorous acid), alcohols (**more active at 70%**), heavy metals, quats (weak vs endospores/mycobacteria; *Pseudomonas* can grow in them), ethylene oxide (sterilant), H₂O₂.

**Disc dilution:** inoculate plate → disinfectant discs → measure zones of inhibition.

**Review misplaced under Ch 7:** dermatophyte diseases belong to **Chapter 12**.

## Chapter 20 — Antibiotics (L7)

**Antibiotic (slide definition):** substance produced by a microbe that, in small amounts, kills/inhibits another microbe. Fleming → antibiosis/antibiotic. Florey & Chain → clinical trials. Ehrlich → selective toxicity; Salvarsan.

**Sources:** natural (*Streptomyces*, *Penicillium*), semisynthetic (ampicillin, amoxicillin), synthetic (trimethoprim). **Review:** which group produces *most*? Slides name sources; they do **not** say “most.”

**Spectrum:** narrow / broad / extended. Adv/disadv: **Not answered**.

**TI = TD50 / ED50.** High TI = safer.

**Five modes of action**

1. Cell wall — penicillins, cephalosporins, bacitracin, vancomycin  
2. Protein synthesis — chloramphenicol, erythromycin, tetracyclines, streptomycin  
3. Nucleic acid — quinolones, rifampin  
4. Plasma membrane — polymyxin B  
5. Metabolite (folate) — sulfanilamide, trimethoprim  

**β-lactams** block peptidoglycan cross-linking. Vancomycin = last line vs MRSA. INH / ethambutol / rifampin for TB (mycolic acid / RNA).

**Resistance:** block entry, efflux, degrade (β-lactamase), alter target (MRSA PBPs), alternate pathway. Superbugs: MRSA, VRE. Drivers: viral colds treated with antibiotics, leftovers, animal feed, incomplete courses. Prevention: only when needed; high concentration; combination; vaccines.

**Kirby-Bauer:** zone of inhibition. **MIC:** lowest concentration preventing growth (E-test).

**Review drug picks** for named pathogens: use only slide assignments. *T. pallidum* / *N. gonorrhoeae* appear with penicillins. TB → INH, ethambutol, rifampin. *S. pyogenes*, *S. pneumoniae*, *M. pneumoniae*: specific oral “first choice” **not listed** on L7. Bactericidal vs bacteriostatic general rule: **Not answered** on L7.

## Chapters 10 & 11 — Bacteria (L8)

Bacteria = prokaryotes (domain Bacteria); this class focuses on pathogens. Identification: morphology, Gram stain, biochemical tests, serology, G+C / sequencing. **Bergey’s Manual** = identification schemes.

**Dichotomous key:** successive two-answer (yes/no) questions for ID. **Cladogram:** evolutionary relationships from shared characters (rRNA/DNA).

**Hierarchy:** Species → Genus → Family → Order → Class → Phylum → Kingdom → Domain. Kingdom **-ati**, phylum **-ota**, class **-ia**, order **-ales**, family **-aceae**.

**Binomial:** *Genus species* (italic). Genus capitalized. Write genus in full the first time (*E. coli* vs *Entamoeba coli*).

**Gram-negative pathogens to know:** *Rickettsia*, *Neisseria*, *Bordetella*, *Pseudomonas*, *Vibrio*, enterics (*E. coli*, *Salmonella*, *Shigella*, *Yersinia*), *Campylobacter*, *H. pylori*, *Chlamydia*, *Borrelia* (Lyme), *Treponema* (syphilis).

**Gram-positive:** low G+C — *Bacillus*, *Staphylococcus*, *Streptococcus*, *Clostridium*, *Mycoplasma* (wall-less). High G+C — *Corynebacterium*, *Mycobacterium*, *Streptomyces* (antibiotic producers), *Gardnerella* (bacterial vaginosis).

## Chapter 12 — Eukaryotes (L9)

**Types:** fungi, algae, protozoa, helminths (worms; not really microbes).

**Fungi:** yeasts (unicellular) / molds (multicellular). Hyphae (septate, coenocytic), mycelium, pseudohyphae. Spores = reproduction; bacterial endospores = survival. Dimorphic: yeast @ 37°C, mold @ 25°C (*C. albicans*).

**Illnesses:** mycoses (tissue infection), mycotoxicoses (toxins), hypersensitivity (farmer’s lung). Cutaneous = dermatophytes / ringworm (*Trichophyton*, *Microsporum*, *Epidermophyton*). Subcutaneous = below skin. Systemic = throughout body. *Candida*: thrush, vaginitis, candidemia. Athlete’s foot = **tinea pedis**, not *Candida* on these slides.

**Protozoa:** trophozoite/cyst; definitive vs intermediate host. *Giardia*, *T. vaginalis* (no cyst; metronidazole), *Toxoplasma* (cat definitive), *Plasmodium* (mosquito definitive; human intermediate).

**Helminths:** *Taenia saginata* (beef), *T. solium* (pork; larvae in tissues), fish tapeworm, *Trichinella*, pinworms (named only).

**Vaginosis vs vaginitis / Gram stain:** BV = *Gardnerella* (Ch 10–11). Yeast vaginitis = *Candida* (here). Gram rule: **Not answered** as a written sentence on Ch 12.

## Where the textbook (Tortora 14e) differs from the slides

Details are in section 4 of each chapter file. These notes come from the textbook’s known content and were not checked against the page text. For the exam, the slide wording comes first.

- **Ch 7:** The book’s sterilization **includes endospores**. Disinfection and antisepsis remove **vegetative** pathogens: disinfection on **nonliving** surfaces, antisepsis on **living** tissue. **Degerming** is only in the book. 70% alcohol works because water helps denature proteins.
- **Ch 20:** The original antibiotic definition was a substance produced by a microbe. Today it also covers semisynthetic and synthetic drugs. **Streptomyces** makes more than half of the antibiotics in use. Broad-spectrum drugs can cause **superinfection**. Cell-wall inhibitors kill (bactericidal). Most protein-synthesis inhibitors only stop growth (bacteriostatic); aminoglycosides are the exception. *Mycoplasma* has no wall, so penicillin does not work.
- **Ch 10–11:** The book may use the old phylum names (Proteobacteria, Firmicutes, Tenericutes, Actinobacteria). The slide uses the new names. L8 slide 18 calls Clostridia “wall-less,” which looks like an error: that row describes *Mycoplasma*.
- **Ch 12:** The book lists **5** mycoses. It adds superficial and opportunistic to the slide’s 3. It defines septate (with cross-walls) vs coenocytic (no cross-walls, many nuclei). The book’s name for *Giardia lamblia* is *G. intestinalis*. Bacterial vaginosis shows clue cells. Yeast vaginitis shows budding Gram-positive yeast.
