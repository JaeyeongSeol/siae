## Summary

This lecture focuses on pathogenic **bacteria** (prokaryotes in the domain Bacteria). Archaea are named as the other prokaryotic domain and then set aside.

**Phylogeny:** Organisms are grouped by common properties. Evidence includes fossils and genomes. Mutations accumulate at a constant rate (molecular clock). Groups evolve from a common ancestor. **rRNA and genome sequencing** are used to determine evolutionary relationships among prokaryotes. **Bergey’s Manual of Determinative Bacteriology** provides identification schemes for bacteria and archaea.

**Identification methods**

- Morphological characteristics (cell wall, shape)
- Differential staining (Gram, capsule)
- Biochemical tests (bacterial enzymes)
- Cellular proteins and fatty acid methyl esters (FEMS)
- Serological methods (antibodies): slide agglutination, ELISA
- Molecular biology: DNA/RNA techniques, metagenomics
- DNA base composition: percent G+C. G+C + A+T = 100%. Closely related organisms have similar base amounts. Next-generation sequencing and NCBI Genome Database are named.

**Dichotomous keys:** successive questions, each with two answers (usually yes/no). Used to identify bacteria. Clinic example: enterics are Gram-negative microbes whose natural habitat is the GI tract; they include pathogens that cause diarrhea. Practice question: unknown produces acids from lactose but cannot grow on citric acid as sole carbon source—the slide asks for the identity and does **not** fill it in.

**Cladograms:** branching diagrams of evolutionary relationships based on shared characteristics (e.g. similarities in rRNA or DNA sequences).

**Hierarchy (2023–2024 updates on the slide):** Species → Genus → Family → Order → Class → Phylum → Kingdom → Domain. Kingdoms for prokaryotes now exist; kingdom names end in **-ati** (Bacillati, Fusobacteriati, Pseudomonadati, Thermotogati). Phyla end in **-ota** (Firmicutes → Bacillota; Bacteroidetes → Bacteroidota). Class ends in **-ia**, order in **-ales**, family in **-aceae**.

**Binomial nomenclature (Linnaeus):** genus (capitalized noun) + specific epithet (lowercase adjective), italicized or underlined. Genus may be abbreviated after first full use (*E. coli*). Why write the genus in full first: *Escherichia coli* and *Entamoeba coli* are different organisms. Trivial names still exist (e.g. coliforms).

- **Eukaryotic species:** closely related organisms that interbreed among themselves  
- **Prokaryotic species:** a population of cells with similar characteristics  
- **Clone:** population of cells derived from a single cell  
- **Strain:** genetically different cells within a species (examples asked; not listed)

Name meanings printed: *Klebsiella pneumoniae*, *Salmonella typhimurium*, *Streptococcus pyogenes*, *Escherichia coli*, *Staphylococcus aureus*.

Gram stain divides bacteria into two broad groups.

### Selected Gram-negative groups (pathogens to know)

| Phylum / group | Notes and pathogens on the slides |
| --- | --- |
| Pseudomonadota (Proteobacteria) — Alpha | *Bartonella*; *Brucella* (brucellosis, zoonosis, unpasteurized milk); *Rickettsia* (*R. rickettsii* Rocky Mountain spotted fever; *R. prowazekii* epidemic typhus; *R. typhi* endemic murine typhus). Also *Ehrlichia*. Low-nutrient growers; *Rhizobium*, *Agrobacterium*, *Wolbachia* |
| Beta | *Neisseria meningitidis* (meningitis); *N. gonorrhoeae* (STIs); *Bordetella pertussis* (whooping cough) |
| Gamma | *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*; *Vibrio cholerae*; enterics: *Escherichia*, *Salmonella*, *Shigella*, *Klebsiella*, *Yersinia* (bubonic plague); also *Haemophilus influenzae* (meningitis in children), *Legionella*, *Coxiella burnetii* (Q fever), *Moraxella*, *Acinetobacter* |
| Campylobacterota | *Campylobacter jejuni* (foodborne intestinal disease); *Helicobacter pylori* (gastritis & peptic ulcers; 30–90% infected; ~15% ulcers; 3% cancer) |
| Chlamydiota | No peptidoglycan; intracellular. Elementary body infective; reticulate bodies reproductive. *C. trachomatis* (trachoma, urethritis); *Chlamydophila psittaci* (psittacosis); *C. pneumoniae* (mild pneumonia in young adults) |
| Bacteroidota | *Bacteroides* in mouth and large intestine (~1 billion/g feces); opportunistic pathogens |
| Spirochaetota | Axial filaments / corkscrew motility. *Borrelia burgdorferi* (Lyme, deer ticks); *Treponema pallidum* (syphilis) |

Enterics on the slide: Gram-negative rods, peritrichous, facultative anaerobes, carbohydrate fermenters.

### Gram-positive groups (by G+C)

**Low G+C (<55%):** Bacillota (Bacilli and Clostridia); Mycoplasmatota (Mollicutes, wall-less).

| Group | Pathogens / notes on the slides |
| --- | --- |
| *Bacillus* | Endospore rods; *B. cereus* (food poisoning) |
| *Staphylococcus* | *S. aureus* (food poisoning, boils, pimples) |
| *Lactobacillus* | Aerotolerant anaerobes; normal vaginal flora |
| *Listeria* | Food poisoning |
| *Streptococcus* | Strep throat, impetigo |
| *Enterococcus* | UTIs, endocarditis |
| *Clostridium* | Obligate anaerobic endospore formers: *C. botulinum*, *C. tetani*, *C. perfringens* |
| *Clostridioides difficile* | Diarrhea and colitis (infection route and predisposing factors asked; not answered beyond naming) |
| *Mycoplasma pneumoniae* | Wall-less, pleomorphic; atypical pneumonia; resistant to many antimicrobials |

**High G+C (>55%):** Actinomycetota — filamentous, often soil, antibiotic producers. *Corynebacterium diphtheriae* (diphtheria); *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*, *M. leprae*; *Cutibacterium acnes* (acne); *Gardnerella vaginalis* (bacterial vaginosis: pain/burning/itching, fishy smell after sex, thin white/gray/green discharge). Deinococcota: *Deinococcus radiodurans* (more radiation-resistant than endospores); *Thermus aquaticus* (Taq polymerase for PCR).

*C. tetani* notes on the slide: spores enter through cuts/wounds; can infect more than once; does not spread person to person; prevention = vaccination and wound care.

## 3. MT2 review questions (exam format)

1. **What are bacteria?** Unicellular prokaryotes in domain Bacteria (this lecture’s focus, especially pathogens).
2. **Key features to identify/classify?** Morphology, differential staining, biochemical tests, serology, DNA base composition / sequencing, dichotomous keys, cladograms.
3. **Dichotomous keys?** Successive two-answer questions for identification.
4. **Cladograms?** Evolutionary relationship maps from shared characters (rRNA/DNA).
5. **Hierarchy with examples?** Use the suffix rules and examples: e.g. Domain Bacteria, Kingdom Pseudomonadati, Phylum Pseudomonadota, Class Gammaproteobacteria, Order Enterobacteriales, Family Enterobacteriaceae, Genus *Escherichia*, Species *E. coli*.
6. **How do we name a microbe?** Binomial: Genus species, italicized.
7. **Major phyla and pathogens?** Use the Gram-negative and Gram-positive tables above.
