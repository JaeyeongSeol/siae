## Summary

Eukaryotic “microbes” on this lecture: **fungi, algae, protozoa, and helminths**. Helminths are multicellular worms and “not really considered microbes,” but they are included as pathogens.

### Prokaryote vs eukaryote (slide says “Responsible for this information”)

| | Prokaryote | Eukaryote |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chromosome | One circular chromosome, not membrane bounded | Paired chromosomes, membrane bounded |
| Histones | No | Yes |
| Organelles | No membrane-bounded organelles | Membrane-bounded organelles |
| Wall | Peptidoglycan (bacteria) | Polysaccharide cell walls |
| Division | Binary fission | Mitotic spindle |

### Fungi

Two forms: unicellular **yeasts** and multicellular **molds**. Most are saprophytic. Benefits named: decompose dead plants, mycorrhizae, food (mushrooms), medicines (antibiotics).

**Terms to know**

- Vegetative structures: thallus (body), hyphae (filaments)
- Septate hyphae vs coenocytic hyphae
- Mycelium = mass of filaments
- Pseudohyphae = incomplete budding, no cytoplasmic connection
- Life cycle: asexual or sexual reproduction by **spores** (reproduction). Contrast with bacterial **endospores** (survival)
- Three medically important phyla: Zygomycota, Ascomycota, Basidiomycota

**Yeasts** are unicellular; budding yeasts divide asymmetrically. **Dimorphic fungi** are yeast-like at 37°C and mold-like at 25°C. Example: *Candida albicans* — candidiasis: superficial (oral thrush, vaginitis) or systemic (candidemia), life-threatening in immunocompromised persons.

**Problems with pathogenic fungi**

| Category | Slide wording / examples |
| --- | --- |
| Mycoses | Infection of body tissues. Aspergillosis, tinea (ringworm), *C. albicans* (mucous membranes, e.g. vaginitis), *Cryptococcus neoformans* (meningitis) |
| Mycotoxicoses | Fungal secondary metabolites toxic at low doses (aflatoxins, fumonisins) |
| Hypersensitivity | Farmer’s lung (*Aspergillus*) |

Fungi produce **keratinase** (degrades keratin in skin, nails, hair).

**Types of mycoses**

- **Cutaneous (dermatophytes):** tinea infections of skin, hair, nails (ringworm). *Trichophyton mentagrophytes* (skin, nails, hair); *Microsporum canis* (skin, nails, hair; major cause of ringworm); *Epidermophyton floccosum* (skin and nails). Tinea capitis (scalp), tinea corporis (body), tinea pedis (athlete’s foot), tinea cruris (upper thigh in males).
- **Subcutaneous:** infections below the skin
- **Systemic:** spread throughout the body

The MT2 review lists the dermatophyte question under Chapter 7. The content is on this Chapter 12 lecture, not on the Chapter 7 control slides.

### Protozoa

About 65,000 species. Complex life cycles may need several hosts.

- **Definitive host:** host in which the parasite matures or reaches reproductive stage  
- **Intermediate host:** harbors a developmental stage (e.g. larva)  
- Forms: **trophozoite** or **cyst**

Asexual reproduction by fission, budding, or **schizogony** (multiple fission: nucleus divides many times before the cell divides).

| Organism | Slide points |
| --- | --- |
| *Giardia lamblia* | Can form cyst; infects intestines — giardiasis (“Beaver fever”) |
| *Trichomonas vaginalis* | STI trichomoniasis; **no cyst**; skin-to-skin; anaerobic flagellated; most common pathogenic protozoan in industrialized countries; women usually symptomatic, men usually asymptomatic; treat with metronidazole |
| *Toxoplasma gondii* | Cats (felids) = definitive hosts; warm-blooded animals = intermediate hosts. Oocyst (developmental, with zygote) vs cyst (survival). Sporozoites → tachyzoites (rapid division) → bradyzoites (slow division). Risk: brain, eyes, fetus (cat litter in pregnancy) |
| *Plasmodium* (malaria) | ~300 million people/year; kills more than HIV in sub-Saharan Africa. Definitive host: *Anopheles* mosquito. Intermediate host: humans |

### Helminths

Reduced digestive system, nervous system, and locomotion; complex reproduction. Dioecious (separate sexes) or monoecious (hermaphrodites). Egg → larva(e) → adult. Flatworms (tapeworms, flukes) and nematodes (roundworms, pinworm).

**Tapeworms (oral–fecal / undercooked meat):** adult in human intestine; eggs in feces ingested by pigs/cattle; larvae lodge in muscle; undercooked meat → adult in human. Attach with hooks; shed proglottids.

| Species | Slide note |
| --- | --- |
| *Taenia saginata* | Beef tapeworm; up to 6 m |
| *Taenia solium* | Pork tapeworm; eggs → larvae in tissues (brain, muscle, eyes) |
| *Diphyllobothrium latum* | Fish tapeworm |

**Roundworms:** trichinosis from raw/undercooked pork or wild game with *Trichinella* larvae (*T. spiralis*). Pinworms: “In-the-clinic” only—no further detail on the slide.

## 3. MT2 review questions (exam format)

1. **Types of eukaryotic microbes?** Fungi, algae, protozoa, helminths (worms included but “not really” microbes).
2. **One example each?** Fungus: *Candida albicans* or a dermatophyte. Protozoan: *Plasmodium* / *Giardia* / *Trichomonas* / *Toxoplasma*. Helminth: *Taenia* or *Trichinella*.
3. **Fungal terms?** See definitions table; spores = reproduction; endospores = bacterial survival.
4. **Mycoses / mycotoxicoses / hypersensitivities?** Tissue infection vs toxin disease vs allergy (farmer’s lung).
5. ***Candida albicans*?** Dimorphic yeast; thrush, vaginitis, candidemia in immunocompromised hosts.
6. **Vaginosis vs vaginitis?** BV = *Gardnerella* (Ch 10–11). Vaginitis from *Candida* = yeast mycosis (this chapter). Gram distinction: review asks it; full stain criteria are not printed on L9.
7. **Cutaneous / subcutaneous / systemic?** Dermatophytes vs below skin vs whole body.
8. **What causes ringworm?** Dermatophytes—*Microsporum canis* is called a major cause; also *Trichophyton* and *Epidermophyton*.

### Terms the slide names and the book defines

- **Septate hyphae:** hyphae divided by cross-walls (septa) into cell-like units, usually one nucleus each.
- **Coenocytic hyphae:** no septa. One long continuous cell with many nuclei.
- **Pseudohyphae:** buds that do not detach and form a short chain. This matches the slide’s “incomplete budding.”
- **Asexual vs sexual reproduction in fungi:** asexual spores form by mitosis from one parent and are genetically identical. Sexual spores form when nuclei from two opposite mating strains fuse.
- **Sexual spores of the three phyla on the slide:** Zygomycota form zygospores. Ascomycota form ascospores. Basidiomycota form basidiospores.
- **Trophozoite:** the active, feeding, growing form of a protozoan.
- **Cyst:** a protective, resting form that survives outside the host.

### *Candida* and athlete’s foot

The book, like the slide, gives **athlete’s foot (tinea pedis)** to the **dermatophytes** (*Trichophyton*, *Epidermophyton*), not to *Candida*. The book describes *Candida albicans* as an **opportunistic** yeast. It causes thrush and vaginitis, often after broad-spectrum antibiotics kill the normal bacteria. That is the superinfection idea from Chapter 20.

### Vaginosis vs vaginitis (review question)

The book covers this in its reproductive-system chapter, which is **not** in the MT2 chapter list:

| | Bacterial vaginosis | Yeast vaginitis (candidiasis) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inflammation | None, which is why it is “-osis” | Yes, which is why it is “-itis” |
| Cause | *Gardnerella vaginalis* | *Candida albicans* |
| Signs | Thin discharge, fishy odor | Itching, thick white discharge |
| Gram stain | **Clue cells**: vaginal cells covered with small Gram-variable rods; few lactobacilli | Large, oval, **budding Gram-positive yeast** cells and pseudohyphae |

### Same in the slides and the book

Dimorphic fungi are yeast-like at 37°C and mold-like at 25°C; dermatophytes cause ringworm (tinea); mycotoxins such as aflatoxin; *Trichomonas* has no cyst stage; cats are the definitive host of *Toxoplasma*; the *Anopheles* mosquito is the definitive host of *Plasmodium* and humans are intermediate; monoecious and dioecious helminths; *Taenia* tapeworms from undercooked beef and pork.
